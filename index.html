Abstract

Empowerment captures the capacity for an agent to actively control its environment. While conceptually appealing as an information-theoretic quantity, the connection between empowerment and MDP centrality has remained an open question. In this work, we link empowerment maximization and skill-learning methods to provide new geometries for interpreting and analyzing empowerment. Our analyses answer longstanding open questions on the connections between empowerment and centrality. Our analysis also reveals distinctions between information and reward geometries, highlighting important theoretical implications to build scalable empowerment-maximization methods.

Key Insight: Empowered States are Central States

An intelligent agent should be in control of its future. A well-trained agent at a mountain can either navigate to and stay at the peak (being in control) or choose and traverse down one of many trails from the peak (actively exerting control). While both useful, these behaviors are distinct.

Empowerment is an information-theoretic object that quantifies controllability in terms of bits. Potential empowerment measures the maximal bits an agent's actions can inform its future state. Effective empowerment quantifies how readily an agent's policy actively informs its future state. In this work, we formally show high potential empowerment states lie at centers of reachable regions in the state occupancy simplex.

Central Insight: Information and Reward Geometries Can Diverge

In tabular settings, empowered agents navigate to central states from which they can adapt to downstream reward functions. However, attempting to extend this result to continuous settings reveals an important insight: information and reward geometries can diverge. An arbitrarily empowered state can provide arbitrarily small adaptation in a continuous MDP with continuous rewards. The same misalignment appears in discrete settings once the reward prior is anisotropic.

Thus, empowered agents are not generally adaptive agents in the continuous setting. Scalable formulations of empowerment maximization must address this discrepancy between information and reward adaptation geometries. Our theoretical results show that unifying empowerment and adaptation requires alternative formulations that align these two geometric perspectives.

Theoretical Contributions

The geometry of empowerment reveals that high-empowerment states occupy central positions in the reachable state space, providing agents maximum control over future outcomes.

Potential and effective empowerment characterize different aspects of controllability: potential empowerment measures accessible control while effective empowerment measures actively exerted control.
